Yes, mar099's own paper settles it, and the answer is tens of kilometres for both moons, Phobos included.

**What it says:** Brozović, Jacobson and Park (2025), the paper describing mar099, Section 6 "Conclusions", page 13 of your PDF. For both satellites, they conclude that the realistic uncertainty is "on the order of a few kilometers for the next couple of decades."

**What that means for the box:** under our rounding rule, "a few kilometres" (anything from 2 to 9 km) earns tens of kilometres, not whole kilometres. So both distances print like "9,380 km" and "23,460 km".

**Why this changes Phobos:** the 2010 paper's "no better than 1 km" described an older version, from before Mars Express's close passes were fitted. In Section 5.3 the 2025 authors also say their computed uncertainties were too optimistic: Phobos's should be scaled up about four times and Deimos's doubled. So the new paper is both the right version and the more honest figure. It replaces the 2010 paper as the citation, and the "cautious bound" caveat goes away.
