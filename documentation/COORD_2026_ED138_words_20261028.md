## 4. The hover's words, approved by Tony Oct 28, 09:11 ("Yes")

One sentence to a bullet; JPL's and the agencies' words kept and
explained; every number the orrery prints comes from its fetched
record, and every sentence below names its source.

- 2026 ED138 is a trans-Neptunian object, a body orbiting beyond
  Neptune, and the first dwarf-planet candidate found in data from the
  Vera C. Rubin Observatory. Announced October 27, 2026. Sources: Rubin
  Observatory news release; Minor Planet Center, MPEC K26U85.
- Matthew Holman of the Center for Astrophysics found it on October
  13, 2026, with search code he wrote with Kevin Napier, in Rubin's
  observations of March 4 to July 11, 2026; older images from the Dark
  Energy Camera then showed it as far back as 2016. Source: the Rubin
  release.
- It goes round the Sun once in about 281 years, between 36.9 and 49.0
  AU, on an orbit tilted 24.5 degrees to the plane of Earth's orbit;
  JPL calls it a hot classical TNO, "hot" for the tilt, which Neptune's
  pull is thought to have given it. Sources: JPL Small-Body Database
  for the numbers; the Rubin release for the class.
- It was last closest to the Sun in 1925 and is now about 46 AU out,
  moving outward. Sources: JPL for the 1925 date; the Rubin release for
  46 AU.
- Its size is not known: 600 to 1,500 km, estimated from its
  brightness and a guessed reflectivity; a body that large would be
  round, which is what makes it a dwarf-planet candidate, and infrared
  observations are needed to settle it. Source: the Rubin release.
- The Pluto comparison (Tony's ask, 09:11): one bullet, every number
  taken from the orrery's own served Pluto record and this object's,
  never typed: its whole orbit lies inside Pluto's range of distances
  from the Sun; its year is longer than Pluto's; its tilt is steeper
  than Pluto's; and it is smaller than Pluto whatever its size turns
  out to be (Pluto's diameter from the store's row, if one exists; if
  none, the bullet says only that Pluto is larger, citing the release's
  600-1,500 km range and the store's Pluto radius). The build writes
  the numbers from the rows and names them in its report.

Left out on purpose: the article's comparison to Makemake and Haumea
