## Confirmed by my own read

- NOAA's discussion of 12 November 2026: status El Nino Advisory; its
  synopsis ("strong-to-very strong El Nino likely through
  January-March 2027 (remaining greater than a 79% chance)"); chances
  of a historic event, defined as "+2.5 C or more for a 3-month RONI
  value", "~60% for the October-December and November-January
  periods"; the next discussion 10 December 2026. It also gives
  "+2.5 C in Nino-3.4" as the latest monthly value (the traditional,
  not the relative, index).
- NOAA's season-by-season chances (cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/
  analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/probabilities/, issued November 2026):
  El Nino 100% OND, NDJ, DJF and JFM; 98% FMA; 80% MAM; 38% AMJ; 12%
  MJJ; 6% JJA. The worksheet's transcription matches mine digit for
  digit. These fill step 4's placeholders.
- NOAA's RONI page: the definition (3-month running mean of ERSST.v6
  SST anomalies in Nino 3.4, 5N-5S, 120-170W, with the tropical mean
  20N-20S subtracted, then adjusted so its variance equals the original
  Nino 3.4 index; threshold +/-0.5 C for five consecutive overlapping
  seasons). Its table: DJF 1983 2.4, NDJ 1982 2.4, NDJ 1997 2.3, NDJ
  2015 2.3; 2026 runs to ASO 2.0.
- NWS Public Information Statement 26-05, issued Jan 13, 2026: RONI
  official "effective February 1, 2026", and "the legacy Nino 3.4 and
  Oceanic Nino Index files will continue to be updated".
- NOAA's RONI handout (roni-info-may-2026.pdf, no date printed in it;
  "May 2026" is the filename): "ONI asks: Is the Nino-3.4 region warmer
  or cooler than average in this region?" and "RONI asks: Is the
  Nino-3.4 region anomaly warmer or cooler than the average tropical
  ocean anomaly?"; and its FAQ, that the 2023-24 El Nino appears
  "weaker" numerically on RONI while "its impacts remain the same", RONI
  removing the part "caused by general oceanic trends".
- L'Heureux and others 2024, Journal of Climate (NOAA repository copy):
  the scaled definition (p. 1200), "the tropical mean index is equally
  driven by ENSO (as measured by ONI) and CO2" (p. 1205), and forecast
  skill "slightly reduced for targets during the first half of the
  year" (abstract).
- NOAA climate.gov, "Where does global warming go during La Nina?":
  "during El Nino, there is less heating of the ocean and more heating
  of the atmosphere than normal. During La Nina, the opposite happens";
  and 0.07 C of global surface warming per 1 C of El Nino warming in
  Nino 3.4, "with a delay of 2-3 months".
- WMO, 3 September 2026: El Nino's impact on global temperatures "can
  ... be most pronounced in the year after development".
- NOAA PMEL, "What is El Nino?": in a normal year "the trade winds pile
  up warm water off the coast of Asia"; "During El Nino ... the trade
  winds relax in the central and western Pacific".
- ECMWF, Tim Stockdale, "Measuring the strength of El Nino", 10 June
  2026: the relative index "is sensitive to conditions outside the
  equatorial Pacific"; removing the warming from the index "does not
  mean that global warming has been removed from the climate system";
  absolute SST "is a key determinant of evaporation, air temperature,
  the transport of moisture" and latent heat.

## What did not hold up

1. **ONI figures (R5, D2, D6).** The worksheet says the traditional ONI
   (ERSST.v6) reached 2.4 in ASO 2026, tying a 2015 peak of 2.4. NOAA's
   ONI v6 table, read Nov 16, says ASO 2026 = 2.5, and 2015 peaked at
   OND 2.5, NDJ 2.6, with DJF 2016 at 2.5. So this event has not passed
   the record on the traditional index either. The worksheet's
   explanation of the news's "record" rests on the wrong numbers.
2. **A quotation not in its source (D6).** "In the past week, the
   Nino-3.4 index reached +2.6 C, while the relative Nino-3.4 index was
   +1.8 C" is not in the 12 November discussion; no sentence there
   mentions a weekly or relative value.
3. **Three quotations not in NOAA's handout (C1, C3, D7).** A search of
   the whole document found no "fixed-base", "combination",
   "gradients", "isolates" or "Walker": the second quotations of C1, C3
   and D7, attributed to it, are not there. Nor is the section heading
   the worksheet cites as their locator ("What is the Difference?: ONI
   vs. RONI"); the document's fourteen headings were listed.
4. **NOAA values from a mirror (C4, R5, R6, D6).** The two-decimal
   values, ASO 2026 = +1.97 among them, came from elnino24.com, an
   educational page, because the leg could not open NOAA's file. On
   Nov 16 NOAA's RONI.ascii.txt still ends at JAS 2026 (+1.69), while
   NOAA's RONI web table shows ASO 2026 = 2.0. Which NOAA file is the
   page's source of record is a question for the build, settled from
   NOAA's own files, not the mirror.

## What changes on the page (leads adopted after my re-open)

- **Step 2, "warmer than usual" (R1).** It describes the old index.
  NOAA's own question for RONI is whether the region is "warmer or
  cooler than the average tropical ocean anomaly". The colours mean
  warmer or cooler than the tropical oceans around it.
- **Step 2, the thin line (R2).** "The gap between the two lines is the
  warming" is wrong: the tropical mean is "equally driven by ENSO ...
  and CO2" (L'Heureux 2024), and the rescaling adds to the gap. If the
  thin line stays, its sentence says the old index includes the
  ocean's general warming, in NOAA's words, and nothing about the gap.
- **Step 3, records (R5).** The page names its index: NOAA's own
  record test is "+2.5 C or more for a 3-month RONI value", with NOAA's
  chance of it (~60% for OND and NDJ). Why the news says "record" is
  not established by this review (point 1 above), so the page does not
  explain the news; it says which index its own line is on.
- **Steps 2 to 4, seasons (R6).** A "season" is a 3-month running
  mean, overlapping, twelve to a year; the words say so once. Step 2
  labels the three strongest EVENTS, 1982-83, 1997-98 and 2015-16.
  Step 4's "record strength" is the discussion's +2.5 C figure, never
  the strength table's 2.0 C column ("very strong").
- **Step 5 (R3, R4, D4, D5).** Its words stand, and now rest on NOAA
  (climate.gov) and WMO, confirmed above. The open question (does
  warming make El Ninos stronger) rests on McPhaden 2023 (NOAA) and Cai
  and others 2023, already read; Coquereau and others 2025 is a lead.
- **Step 1 (D3).** Rests on NOAA PMEL, confirmed above.
