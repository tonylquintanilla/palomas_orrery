## Ceres

**The quoted NASA sentences: NOT FOUND word for word.**
- Page: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dawn/science/ceres/ , title "Ceres - NASA Science".
- The first quoted sentence is only part of a longer one. The page reads: "Ceres was the first object discovered in the main asteroid belt and is named for the Roman goddess of agriculture."
- The second quoted sentence, "Dawn spacecraft orbited Ceres from 2015 to 2018.", is not on the page in any wording. The page gives only a Mission Events list: "March 5, 2015: Ceres Arrival" and "Nov. 1, 2018: End of Mission".
- So the description shows as a NASA quotation something NASA did not write.

**"orbited Ceres from 2015 to 2018": DIFFERS.**
- 2015 is the arrival year, and that part is right. 2018 is when the mission ended, not when the orbit ended. The spacecraft is still in orbit.
- Page: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/text/dawn_pr_20181101.txt , headline "NASA's Dawn Mission to Asteroid Belt Comes to End" (NASA press release, 1 November 2018).
  - It says: "Currently, it's in orbit around the dwarf planet Ceres, where it will remain for decades."
  - It also says: "In 2015, when Dawn went into orbit around Ceres, a dwarf planet that is also the largest world in the asteroid belt," (partial)
- Two NASA pages disagree on the arrival day:
  - The Ceres body page (https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/ceres/ , title "Ceres") says: "NASA's Dawn mission went into orbit around Ceres on March 6, 2015," (partial).
  - The Dawn Ceres page's events list says "March 5, 2015: Ceres Arrival".
  - The year is the same on both, so this does not matter for the text unless a day is given.
- The Dawn mission page (https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dawn/ , title "Dawn - NASA Science") gives neither year.

**Link:** https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dawn/science/ceres/ loads and is about Ceres, so it passes the rule. There is also a NASA page for Ceres itself, which I opened: https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/ceres/ , title "Ceres". It is the better choice if one link has to be picked.

## Haumea

**Rotation "3.9 hours": CONFIRMED (from a page that names its source, plus the paper itself).**
- Page: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haumea , title "Haumea".
  - It says: "Haumea displays large fluctuations in brightness over a period of 3.9 hours," / " which can only be explained by a rotational period of this length." (two pieces)
  - That sentence cites an AFP 2009 news item. The infobox gives "3.915341+/-0.000005 h (0.163139208 d)" and shows no citation next to it.
- Primary paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0509401 , "Photometric Observations Constraining the Size, Shape, and Albedo of 2003 El61, a Rapidly Rotating, Pluto-Sized Object in the Kuiper Belt" (Rabinowitz, Barkume, Brown, Roe, Schwartz, Tourtellotte, Trujillo).
  - The abstract says: "with period 3.9154 +/- 0.0002 hours and peak to peak amplitude 0.28 +/- 0.04 mag." (partial)
- The entry's own link rounds it differently. https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/haumea/ , title "Haumea", says: "As Haumea orbits the Sun, it completes one rotation every 4 hours" (partial). "3.9" does not appear on that page.

**"Egg-shaped": DIFFERS.**
- Neither page says "egg". The fetch confirmed the word does not appear on the NASA page or in the Wikipedia article.
- NASA page says: "Haumea is an oval-shaped dwarf planet that is one of the fastest rotating large objects in our solar system." and "The fast spin distorts Haumea's shape, making this dwarf planet look like a football."
- Wikipedia says: "Haumea rotates so quickly that it is distorted into a triaxial ellipsoid."
- Wording the sources support: "oval-shaped" (NASA), or "football-shaped" (NASA's comparison).

**"With rings": CONFIRMED.**
- NASA page says: "Haumea is the first known Kuiper Belt Object to have rings." and "Scientists announced the discovery in 2017 after watching the dwarf planet pass in front of a star."

**Link:** https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/haumea/ loads, is about Haumea, and passes.
