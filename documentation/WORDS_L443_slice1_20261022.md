## Settled by the rules, reported

- **Wierzchos moves to the spacecraft slice.** JPL now classes it a
  hyperbolic comet, so it is on an open path like the other thirteen.
  Slice 1 is 50 bodies.
- **Orcus and Patroclus are served by their small-body records**
  (2004 DW and A906 UL), which JPL already computes for the pair's
  shared centre ("integrated barycenter"). Their shared-centre numbers
  would have turned up in both of JPL's indexes and turned your
  Horizons check red.
- **The shared centre lies outside the bigger body for two pairs:**
  Orcus-Vanth (about 1,250 km out, Orcus's radius about 455 km) and
  Patroclus-Menoetius (about 150 km out, radius about 59 km). These
  two get the sentence Pluto has. For Eris, Haumea, Makemake,
  Gonggong and Quaoar it lies inside, so their symbol is the body and
  carries no sentence. The sources are in the check file.
- **Each body's group in the drawer is JPL's own class** (its
  Small-Body Database), not our judgement.
- **Numbers that came out:** orbit figures the website already serves
  from JPL (comet periods, perihelion dates and distances); moon orbit
  times (they belong with the moons' slice); and any number no open
  page states as we had it.
- **Two errors fixed:** Chariklo was called retrograde; JPL gives an
  inclination of 23.4 degrees, so it goes around the Sun the same way
  as the planets. Mani was called "unnumbered"; it is 307261 Mani.
- **One duplicate recorded, not touched:** the orrery entry
  "6AC4721" is MAPS under its temporary name (Wikipedia: "formerly
  known by its temporary designation as 6AC4721"). Its id is "none".

  - Proposed: A Jupiter-family comet, discovered in 1873 by Wilhelm
    Tempel.
  - Why: the period is served from JPL. Deep Space 1 flew past comet
    Borrelly in 2001, not Tempel 2 (NASA's Borrelly page).
- **Schaumasse**
  - Now: Jupiter-family comet, 8.25-year period. Discovered 1911.
    Perihelion Jan 8, 2026 at 1.18 AU.
  - Proposed: A Jupiter-family comet, discovered in 1911.
- **Howell**
  - Now: Jupiter-family comet, 5.5-year period. Discovered 1981 by
    Ellen Howell. Perihelion Mar 18, 2026.
  - Proposed: A Jupiter-family comet, discovered in 1981 by Ellen
    Howell.
- **Churyumov-Gerasimenko**: 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko is the comet
  visited by the Rosetta spacecraft, August 2014 through September

- **Hale-Bopp**: Visible to the naked eye for a record 18 months.
- **Hyakutake**
  - Now: Retrograde. Passed very close to Earth in 1996. Retrograde

  - Proposed: Passed very close to Earth in 1996. It goes around the

  - Link: NASA's Hyakutake page (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov), not the Ulysses

- **Ikeya-Seki**
  - Now: Retrograde. One of the brightest comets of the 20th century.

  - Proposed: One of the brightest comets seen in the last thousand

  - Why: Wikipedia's wording. Link: decision 1.
- **NEOWISE**
  - Now: Retrograde. Brightest comet visible from the Northern

  - Proposed: The brightest comet seen from the Northern Hemisphere

  - Link: JPL's 2020 article on the comet, not the NEOWISE telescope's

- **Lemmon**
  - Now: Retrograde. In Fall 2025, Comet Lemmon is brightening and

  - Proposed: In fall 2025 it brightened in the morning northern sky.

### Jupiter Trojans (5)

Diameters come out of all four Lucy targets not yet visited: the
pages give different estimates (Eurybates 64 km on SwRI's page,
64 to 72 km on Wikipedia's), and Lucy will measure them. Links: each
body's Wikipedia article, since NASA has no page for any of them and
SwRI's pages are neither NASA's nor Wikipedia's.

- **Eurybates**
  - Now: Lucy flyby: Aug 12, 2027. D~64 km, C-type. Has satellite
    Queta (~1 km). First C-type Trojan to be visited.
  - Proposed: Lucy flies past it on August 11, 2027. A C-type
    (carbon-rich) asteroid with a small moon, Queta.
  - Why: NASA and SwRI both give August 11. No page says "first
    C-type Trojan to be visited".
- **Patroclus and Menoetius**
  - Now (the orrery's Patroclus): Primary body of binary Trojan.
    D=113 km. Binary period: 4.28 days. Lucy flyby: March 3, 2033.
  - Proposed: Two Jupiter Trojans of nearly the same size, Patroclus
    and Menoetius, orbiting each other. Lucy flies past them in March
    2033.
  - The drawer's sentence, as Pluto's: "The symbol marks the center of
    mass (barycenter) that Patroclus and Menoetius orbit together. It
    lies outside Patroclus itself."
- **Polymele**
  - Now: Lucy flyby: Sep 15, 2027. D~21 km, P-type. Has satellite
    Shaun (~5 km, discovered 2022 via occultation).
  - Proposed: Lucy flies past it in September 2027. A P-type
    (primitive) asteroid with a small moon, Shaun, found in 2022 when
    Polymele passed in front of a star.
  - Why: NASA gives September 14 (Eastern time), Wikipedia September
    15, so the month only.
- **Leucus**
  - Now: Lucy flyby: Apr 18, 2028. D~40 km, D-type. Extremely slow
    rotator (~446 hours). Dark, primitive surface.
  - Proposed: Lucy flies past it on April 18, 2028. A D-type asteroid
    with a dark, primitive surface, which rotates very slowly.
  - Why: the rotation figure differs within its own source (445.7
    hours in one place, 466 in another).
- **Orus**
  - Now: Lucy flyby: Nov 11, 2028. D~51 km, D-type. Last L4 Trojan
    visit before heading to L5.
  - Proposed: Lucy flies past it on November 11, 2028, its last stop
    among the Trojans that lead Jupiter. A D-type asteroid.

### Trans-Neptunian objects (15, beside Pluto)

- **Orcus**
  - Now: Dwarf planet with moon Vanth. Binary period: 9.54 days.
    Highest mass ratio (~16%) of any known binary.
  - Proposed: A dwarf planet with a large moon, Vanth, about 16% of
    its mass: the highest share of any known planet or dwarf planet
    and its moon.
  - Why: Brown and Butler (2023), on arXiv, give 0.16 and that
    comparison. The moon's orbit time goes with the moons.
  - The drawer's sentence: "The symbol marks the center of mass
    (barycenter) that Orcus and its moon Vanth orbit together. It lies
    outside Orcus itself."
- **Haumea**
  - Now: Egg-shaped dwarf planet with rings. Moon periods: Hi'iaka 49
    days, Namaka 18 days. Rotation: 3.9 hours.
  - Proposed: A dwarf planet with rings, spinning about once every 4
    hours, fast enough to stretch it into an oval like a football.
  - Why: NASA's page, which is its link, says "oval-shaped", "look
    like a football" and "every 4 hours"; no page says "egg".
- **Eris**
  - Now: Most massive dwarf planet (27% more than Pluto). Binary period
    with Dysnomia: 15.79 days.
  - Proposed: The most massive known dwarf planet, about the same size
    as Pluto.
  - Why: the 27% comes from a paywalled paper (Brown and Schaller
    2007). NASA gives "about the same size as Pluto".
- **Makemake**: Makemake is a dwarf planet slightly smaller than Pluto,
  and is the second-brightest object in the Kuiper Belt.
- **Gonggong**
  - Now: Dwarf planet with highly inclined orbit. Binary period with
    Xiangliu: 25.22 days.
  - Proposed: Dwarf planet with a highly inclined orbit.
- **Quaoar**
  - Now: Kuiper Belt dwarf planet with ring system (discovered 2023).
    Binary period with Weywot: 12.44 days.
  - Proposed: Kuiper Belt dwarf planet with a ring system, announced in
    2023.
  - Why: the rings were found in observations from 2018 to 2021 and
    announced in 2023. Link: Wikipedia; NASA's old address now lands on
    its general planets page.
- **Sedna**: A distant trans-Neptunian dwarf planet with an extremely
  long orbit.
  - Link: Wikipedia, as for Quaoar.
- **Mani**
  - Now: One of the largest unnumbered Kuiper Belt Objects with no
    known moons.
  - Proposed: One of the largest Kuiper Belt objects with no known
    moons.
  - Link: Wikipedia, not the Minor Planet Center's database page.
- **Ixion**: A large Kuiper Belt object without a known moon.
- **Varuna**: A significant Kuiper Belt Object with a rapid rotation
  period.
- **Goibniu (2004 GV9)**: A binary Kuiper Belt Object providing precise
  mass measurements through its moon.
  - Link: Wikipedia's "90568 Goibniu" (the old address now redirects
    there).
- **Leleakuhonua**: A distant trans-Neptunian dwarf planet with an
  extremely long orbit.
- **2017 OF201**
  - Now: A extreme trans-Neptunian object with an extremely long orbit.
  - Proposed: An extreme trans-Neptunian object with an extremely long
    orbit.
- **2023 KQ14 (Ammonite)**: Ammonite is classified as a sednoid, after
  Sedna.
- **Arrokoth**: Arrokoth flyby from New Horizons on January 1, 2019.
  - Link: Wikipedia; NASA's only Arrokoth page is a single 3D image.
