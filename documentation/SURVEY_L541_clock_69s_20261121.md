# Survey: 69 seconds of motion against each box's printed error (L-541)

Built on gallery 7d1e2e16 at
https://github.com/tonylquintanilla/tonyquintanilla.github.io (branch
main), with orrery ae9ec0f9 at
https://github.com/tonylquintanilla/palomas_orrery. Measured November
21, 2026 by the coordinating session (Claude Fable 5.2). A record of
one measurement; nothing reads this file.

## What was asked

L-541 (a plan's clock) carried a question marked not verified: whether
the live rooms draw every body 69 seconds from the minute shown, and
whether any box prints finer than that. The trace is on L-541. This is
the second half: the sizes.

## How it was measured

- The moment: Julian date 2461366.4167, which is 22:00 UTC on
  November 21, 2026, about 0.92 days after the served orbits' date.
- A body worked out from its orbit (trust method two_body_rate_v1):
  its place from `gallery/assembler/render_orbits.propagate_marker` at
  that Julian date and at 69.184 seconds before it.
- A body worked out from served points: its place from
  `gallery/assembler/render_spacecraft.point_at`, the same two moments,
  with the curve's error where the page uses it.
- "moves km" is the distance between the two places. "dist chg km" is
  the change in its distance from its centre, which is the figure a
  box prints.
- "error km" re-works the page's rule (`gallery/solar_system_figures.js`,
  `distanceLine` and `pathDistanceLine`) in Python: the larger of the
  drift, JPL's own figure (a small body's `range_3sigma_km`, or half a
  unit of a planet's `position_accuracy` place) and one kilometre; for
  served points, the larger of the working-out's error and one
  kilometre. "prints to" is the place that error earns.
- A row is marked where the distance changes by more than the error.

## What it is not

- Not the page's own code run in a browser: the rule was re-worked
  here, so a row near the line could fall either side.
- One moment. The drift grows through the day and a body's speed
  toward its centre changes along its orbit, so the count is for that
  hour.
- A moon's row uses its distance from its planet.
- Eight of 114 were not worked out: the Sun, and seven ended missions
  whose served points stop before that day.
