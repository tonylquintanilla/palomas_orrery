## What this is, and what it is not

This is a READ, not a confirmation. We are not asking you to check our
numbers, and we have deliberately not told you what they are. Go to
each source, report what it says, and stop there.

That constraint is the whole point. If we handed you our figure and
asked whether it was right, your answer would be contaminated by ours
and we would learn nothing we did not already believe. An independent
read is the only thing that can actually disagree with us. So please
do not ask what we currently hold, and if you happen to know what a
visualization project would typically store for one of these, set it
aside.

You are also not being asked to reconcile anything, resolve a
disagreement, or recommend a value. Where two sources conflict, report
both and let the conflict stand. Deciding between defensible
measurements is our call and we want it made with the split visible.

## How to answer

For each source, per quantity:

- **The figure as published**, with its uncertainty.
- **Whether it is QUOTED or DERIVED** -- stated in the document, or
  computed by you from something else in it. We annotate those
  differently, so the distinction matters more to us than the number.
- **Where in the document** it appears, closely enough that someone
  could find it again.
- **The full citation** as it should be written -- authors, year,
  journal, volume, page. Several of the references below are given
  loosely on purpose; part of the job is telling us the correct form.

If you cannot reach a source, say so and skip it. If a figure is not
recoverable from the primary document, say that rather than supplying a
plausible one. A blank with a reason is more useful to us than a number
we cannot trace back.

## Group 1 -- Earth's equatorial radius

**1. IERS Conventions (Petit & Luzum 2010).** What equatorial radius
does it publish for Earth, at full stated precision? Give the exact
figure and the section or table.

**2. IAU 2015 Resolution B3**, and its companion paper (given to us
loosely as Prsa et al. 2016, AJ 152:41 -- correct it if wrong). What
nominal terrestrial equatorial radius does B3 define, at what
precision, and is it presented as a measurement or as a conversion
constant?

Between these two: if they differ in the digits they publish, say by
how much and which one carries more.

## Group 2 -- Helmet streamers and the solar corona

**3. Golub & Pasachoff, "The Solar Corona" (2010).** What does it give
for the radial extent of helmet streamers, in solar radii? Report the
range as stated and whether it is presented as typical, observed, or
bounded.

**4. DeForest, Howard & McComas (2014), ApJ 787:124** (correct the
citation if wrong). Two questions, and the second is the one that
matters most to us:

  - What does the paper report about the radial extent of streamer-belt
    structure?
  - The figure 6 solar radii appears in connection with this work.
    What role does it play in the paper -- an upper bound, a lower
    bound, a detection threshold, an instrument limit, or something
    else? Quote the sentence that establishes it.

We are asking because a bound can be cited in the wrong direction and
the citation still looks correct.

## Group 3 -- Bennu

**5. Nolan et al. 2013**, the pre-encounter radar shape model. What
mean diameter or mean radius does it give, with uncertainty? Give the
correct full citation.

**6. Any OSIRIS-REx mission publication reporting Bennu's global shape**
from the laser altimeter (OLA) or from imaging. Does the mission
independently publish a mean or volume-equivalent radius? If so, give
it with uncertainty and the citation. If the mission's published figure
turns out to restate the earlier radar result rather than derive a new
one, say that explicitly -- that is a distinction we care about.

## Group 4 -- Haumea

**7. The 2017 stellar occultation of Haumea** (we have a citation for
it but are withholding it; give us the correct one). Report the
triaxial dimensions as published, and state explicitly whether they are
SEMI-axes or full axial lengths. That distinction changes the result by
a factor of two and is routinely lost in secondary sources. Does the
paper publish a mean, equivalent or volumetric radius directly, or
would one have to be computed from the axes?

**8. Lockwood et al. 2014.** Same questions -- dimensions, semi-axes or
full, and whether a mean radius is published directly.

**9. JPL Small-Body Database, current entry for Haumea.** What mean
radius does it publish today, and which shape solution does that figure
come from? If the database figure predates the occultation, say so.

## One request about scope

Answer only what is asked. If reading these turns up something you
think we would want to know -- a retraction, a superseding measurement,
an error in a widely-copied secondary source -- add it at the end under
a heading of its own, clearly separated from the answers. We would
rather have it than not, but we want to be able to tell which part of
your reply is the read and which part is yours.
