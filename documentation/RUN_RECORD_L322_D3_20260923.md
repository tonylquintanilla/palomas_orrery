## What the patch changes

Three new files and four edited ones, all or nothing.

- **`earth_pole_of_date.py`, new.** For the plot's date it fetches
  Earth's north pole (Horizons observer quantity 32, target 399, seen
  from the Sun; astroquery's columns `NPole_RA` and `NPole_DEC`, checked
  in astroquery 0.4.11's own source) and the Earth-Moon barycenter's
  osculating orbit (target 3 about the Sun, ecliptic). It computes the
  tilt as the angle between the two, after turning the pole into the
  drawing's frame with `EARTH_OBLIQUITY_J2000_DEG`. It caches both, with
  their source blocks, in `data/earth_pole_cache.json`, keyed by the day.
  When Horizons cannot be reached, or no date is given, it returns the
  fallback pair from `constants_new.py` and no tilt.
- **`test_earth_pole_of_date.py`, new.** 14 offline checks; added to the
  maintenance run as "Earth pole of date".
- **`earth_pole_live_check.py`, new.** The script Tony runs to see real
  fetches. It writes nothing.
- `idealized_orbits.py`: `create_planet_transformation_matrix('Earth')`
  reads the pole of the scene's date. Every other body is unchanged.
- `planet_visualization_utilities.py`: Earth's axis hover prints the
  tilt of date, or says why none is shown. The typed `23.44 deg` goes.
- `palomas_orrery.py`: the static plot sets the scene date to its date,
  the animation to its first frame's date, just before the center-body
  shells are built. The social export reuses the last plotted figure and
  needs no change. The two consumers in the gallery repository render
  figures already built and fetch nothing (Check All Parallel Pipelines).
- `orrery_maintenance_run.py`: the new checker line.

## Hover text, before and after

Earth's rotation axis marker, the Obliquity line:

- Before: `Obliquity: 23.44 deg`
- After, with a date and a fetch (the manifest's 2026-01-01 pole):
  `Obliquity: 23.43598 deg on 2026-01-01,` / `measured against the
  Earth-Moon orbit that day (JPL Horizons).` / `It includes Earth's small
  nod, so it differs slightly` / `from the smoothed textbook value. The
  axis also circles` / `slowly over thousands of years`
- After, with no connection: `Obliquity: not shown: Horizons could not
  be reached,` / `so the axis drawn is the frame's axis, Earth's average
  pole of the year 2000`

The slashes mark the line breaks in the hover. The tilt prints at seven
figures: Horizons prints the pole's declination to five decimal places,
seven figures, and states no uncertainty, so Rule 3's counting fallback
sets the count.
