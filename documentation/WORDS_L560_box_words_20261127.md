## The shape (Tony, 20:36: "yes")

- In the box, the usual case: the distance and no sentence. Four
  figures, both units.
- In the box, one sentence only when it shows fewer than four figures,
  or none. The sentence gives the true reason.
- In the information panel: both sources, the position's and the
  uncertainty's. The position's is there today.
- On the page, once: the standing sentence, and what "au" means.

Said to Tony with it: statement 5 of the rule, "The box names the
position's source and what the rounding rests on", is then met by the
box with its information panel, not by the box alone.

How four boxes would read, from the served figures of that night:
- Jupiter: 5.325 au from the Sun (796.6 million km)
- Voyager 1: 172.5 au from the Sun (25.80 billion km)
- The Moon: 360,000 km from Earth (0.00241 au), with sentence 6.
- Io: four figures, with sentence 5 (and see "Not settled" below).

## On the page, once

1. APPROVED (Tony, 21:22: "approved"), as reworded after his word of
   20:53 that the first wording, "that is our choice, not a statement
   of accuracy", "sounds arbitrary, when we are actually being
   conservative":
   "Distances are measured from centre to centre and shown to at most
   four significant figures. Most are known more finely than that; we
   round so the figures are easy to read and never claim more than is
   known. A distance shows fewer figures where the evidence supports
   fewer."
   "Most" was measured that night: of the 64 bodies on orbits with a
   sourced uncertainty, 60 are known more finely than their fourth
   figure. The build measures it again before the sentence ships.

2. HELD FOR ITS SOURCE:
   "au is the astronomical unit: 149,597,870.7 km, about Earth's
   average distance from the Sun."
   The kilometre figure is the IAU's (2012 Resolution B2, checked at
   its own page on Nov 27, on L-560). "About Earth's average distance
   from the Sun" is the session's gloss with no source read: it gets a
   source before the build, or it comes out.

3. OUT (Tony, 20:53: "omit"): "A billion is a thousand million."

## In a box, when it shows fewer than four figures

4. TAKEN AS APPROVED (put to Tony before his reply of 20:53, where he
   passed over it; he was told it would be read so before his reply
   of 21:22, and did not object). New. For a body on a path, when the
   page's own error costs it figures:
   "This distance is rounded to how far this page's working-out
   between JPL's points may be off at the minute shown."

6. TAKEN AS APPROVED, the same way. Reused: a moon's box prints it
   today (`childBox`, the website's `gallery/solar_system_figures.js`).
   For a body on an orbit, when drift costs it figures:
   "This distance is rounded to how far this page's working-out from
   JPL's orbit may have drifted by the minute shown."

7. TAKEN AS APPROVED, the same way. Reused from the orrery's hover
   (`position_accuracy._distance_sentence`), "These distances" made
   "This distance". For an asteroid or comet whose JPL uncertainty is
   coarser than four figures:
   "This distance is rounded to JPL's uncertainty for [name]'s position
   on this date, at three standard deviations."

11. HELD FOR THE 12 ROWS' CHECK. Reused from a moon's box:
   "This distance is rounded to JPL's published accuracy for [Phobos's
   orbit around Mars]."
   It is true only if the row behind it is JPL's stated accuracy and
   not the project's own rounding place (the rule's statement 5).

## In a box, under the interim: "B until we have A" (Tony, 21:02)

Until a body's source is read, its box prints four figures and says
what they rest on. Both sentences APPROVED (Tony, 21:22: "approved").
They replace the first drafts of 5 and 8, which printed no number; of
those Tony said, 20:53: "we can't say that we don't know these
distances. we do." and "again, we do know the distance."

5. For a body whose source we have not read (Io, Titan, Pluto and
   Charon that night):
   "Worked out from JPL's orbit and shown to four figures. We have not
   yet read a source for how finely this position is known."

8. For a body where JPL answered and gave none (Bennu, Halley,
   Ikeya-Seki; and the eight Lagrange points, whose box says so today):
   "Worked out from JPL's orbit and shown to four figures. JPL states
   no uncertainty for this position."
   NOT CHECKED: a Lagrange point is placed from served points, not
   from an orbit, so "Worked out from JPL's orbit" is not true of it
   as written. Its opening words are for the build's brief and Tony.

## In a box, when no number is shown

Both TAKEN AS APPROVED, as 4, 6 and 7 were. Reused from the website's
sentences of today, with "No distance is shown:" put in front. The
interim does not reach them: JPL has an uncertainty there, and it may
be coarse.

9. A question to JPL that failed, or was never asked:
   "No distance is shown: JPL's own uncertainty for this position is
   not yet included."

10. An answer that did not match:
   "No distance is shown: JPL was asked for its own uncertainty for
   this position, but its answer did not match this position."

## Not settled, and not put to Tony

- The spacecraft's line in the information panel. The tracking
  evidence covers only the part of a path fitted to tracking data; for
  a craft still flying, today is usually past JPL's last fit. It waits
  on L-554 (JPL's sentence when a path is a prediction).
- Whether the interim reaches a craft on a predicted part of its path.
- The orrery's hover. It has no information panel, so the shape above
  does not fit it as it stands.
- A body that is under the interim and also loses figures to drift
  (Io, most hours of the day): sentence 5, sentence 6, or both.
- The wording of the information panel's second source line, the
  uncertainty's.
