## 1. The day each mission ended

Each is the date on the agency's own page (the brief's rule), quoted
on the orrery's entry as `mission_end`. JPL's last day is beside it.

| Craft | Mission end | The page says | JPL's last day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pioneer 10 | 23 January 2003 | NASA: "on Jan. 23, 2003, it sent its last signal" | 1 January 2050 |
| Pioneer 11 | 24 November 1995 | NASA: "The mission ended in 1995"; "Scientists later received a few minutes of good engineering data on Nov. 24, 1995" | 1 January 2050 |
| Rosetta | 30 September 2016 | ESA: "Mission end: 30 September 2016" | 5 October 2016 |
| DART | 26 September 2022 | NASA: "On Sept. 26, 2022, DART impacted the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos" | runs on, past 2022 |
| Akatsuki | 18 September 2025 | JAXA: operations ended "from 9 a.m. on September 18, 2025" (translated) | 24 August 2025, but see section 6 |

- Pioneer 10's page gives three dates and calls none the end: routine
  contact ended 31 March 1997, the last data came 27 April 2002, the
  last signal 23 January 2003. I took the last signal, the last day
  anyone heard from it. The page's own "Pioneer 10 lasted more than 30
  years" fits only that one.
- Pioneer 11 by the same rule: the last day anyone heard from it.
  Its page also gives 30 September 1995, when NASA Ames "made last
  contact". Say if you want that date instead.
- These two Pioneer dates are my proposal, not yet your ruling.
- Pages: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/pioneer-10/,
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/pioneer-11/,
  https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Rosetta,
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dart/,
  https://www.jaxa.jp/press/2025/09/20250918-2_j.html (Japanese; JAXA's
  English page refused to be read). All read on November 5, 2026.
- Each path stops on that day, except Akatsuki's (section 6). Rosetta's

Each replaces the orrery's own `mission_info`, so the desktop orrery
reads them too.

- Pioneer 10. Now: "First spacecraft to travel through the asteroid
  belt and make direct observations of Jupiter." Proposed: "Pioneer 10
  was NASA's first mission to the outer planets, the first spacecraft
  to fly through the main asteroid belt and the first to fly past
  Jupiter." NASA's page: "Pioneer 10 was NASA's first mission to the
  outer planets", and among its firsts "First spacecraft to fly through
  the main asteroid belt" and "First spacecraft to fly past Jupiter".
- Pioneer 11. Now: "First spacecraft to encounter Saturn and study its
  rings." Proposed: "Pioneer 11, the sister spacecraft of Pioneer 10,
  was the first spacecraft to study Saturn up close." NASA's page, the
  same words.
- Rosetta. Now: "European Space Agency mission to study Comet
  67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko." Proposed: "ESA's Rosetta was the first
  mission to travel with a comet, Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko, and
  the first to set a lander down on one." ESA's page: "the first to
  rendezvous with a comet, the first to follow a comet on its orbit
  around the Sun, and the first to deploy a lander to a comet's
  surface."
- DART. Now: "NASA's mission to test asteroid deflection." Proposed:
  "NASA's DART was the first mission to test a way of turning an
  asteroid aside. It struck Dimorphos, a small moon of the asteroid
  Didymos." NASA's page: "DART was the first-ever mission dedicated to
  investigating and demonstrating one method of asteroid deflection",
  and the moonlet Dimorphos "orbits a larger ... asteroid called
  Didymos".
- Akatsuki. Now: "JAXA mission to study the atmospheric circulation of
  Venus". Proposed: "JAXA's Venus Climate Orbiter Akatsuki studied the
  atmosphere of Venus from orbit, from December 2015." JAXA's release:
  it entered Venus orbit in December 2015 and observed the atmosphere
  of Venus for more than 8 years.
