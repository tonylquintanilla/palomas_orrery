## The fault

BepiColombo entered orbit around Mercury on 21 November 2026 on an
orbit that is not the planned one. JPL's path for it is fitted to data
only through 17 November and is the plan after that. The website draws
that plan as the craft's path and prints its distance from it.

## The ruling

17 November 2026, the last day JPL's path is fitted to data, as
Akatsuki's is cut at the last day JPL's solution is fitted to tracking
(his ruling of 2026-11-05, L-507). The cut and these words come off
together when JPL fits its path past the arrival.

## The seven sentences, in the order the box gives them

For a craft whose entry has no `mission_end` and has a `path_cutoff`,
when the moment shown is past its path's last point. BepiColombo's
days.

1. "The path is drawn to 17 November 2026, the last day JPL's path is
   fitted to data, and the marker is where it was that day."
2. "JPL calls its path after that day a prediction, and it is not
   drawn."
3. "ESA reports that the entry burn ended early and the craft is in a
   75-hour orbit, not the planned 58-hour one; JPL's prediction still
   shows the planned orbit."
4. "It entered orbit around Mercury on 21 November 2026."
5. After the distance figure: " on the last day drawn" (Akatsuki's
   words, `ENDED_EARLY_DISTANCE_WORDS`, reused).
6. The sentence about the line: "Its path is solid all the way, since
   the day shown is past the last day drawn, from 21 October 2018 to
   17 November 2026."
7. The end of the source line: ", as far as JPL's data is fitted".

Sentences 1 to 4 are BepiColombo's own. Sentences 5 to 7 are for any
craft still flying whose path is cut. Which are kept on the orrery's
entry and which on the page is the build's to say; the words are
these either way.

  the entry dated 23 November 2026,
  https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/Latest_updates_BepiColombo_s_arrival_at_Mercury
  read through the same pane, 14:12 Chicago. Its words on the orbit:
  the craft "has entered a 75-hour orbit, rather than the originally

  November, 58.7 to 58.9 hours; 99,786 to 99,843 seconds from 27
  November, 27.7 hours, the plan's next burn.
- 21 October 2018: the path's first day as the room prints it today.

## What the words leave out

They do not say where the craft is now, or that JPL's path is wrong:
only what is drawn, what JPL calls the rest, and what ESA reports.
Tony, 2026-11-05, on Akatsuki's: "Box wording that describes what is
drawn and does not certify JPL's accuracy".

## When they are retired

Sentence 3's second half is true while JPL's path file is the one
named above. So the night watches JPL's sheet for -121: its fit date
and its path file's name. A change in either is reported so it cannot
be missed, and a session then reads the new path, takes the cut off
the entry and these words with it. Sentence 4 stays true; whether it
stays in the box is asked then.
