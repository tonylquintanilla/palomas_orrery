## Tony's ruling, Oct 26, 11:44

"we should always keep the correct JPL words and explain them when
needed."

## What it means (Claude's reading, for the build session)

- **The term stays; the gloss is added.** Where JPL, the IAU or
  another authority has a word for a thing, the visitor reads that
  word, and a plain explanation follows it when the word is not
  everyday English. "Hyperbolic" is not replaced by "open path"; it
  reads "Hyperbolic: an open path around the Sun, not a closed loop."
  The same for "osculating", "perihelion", "barycentre", "Keplerian",
  "near-parabolic", JPL's class names (HYP, COM, Apollo, Amor, Centaur,
  TNO) and the IAU's "I" designations for interstellar objects.
- **Why.** The authority's word is what a visitor will meet everywhere
  else, in JPL's pages, in the agency's story, in a search. A site that
  renames it teaches a private vocabulary. Keeping it is also
  provenance: the word is part of what was read, and the source line
  the i panel prints uses it.
- **The explanation is for a visitor, in plain words**, under the
  rules already in force: no project words, no numbers that are not
  served, and the gloss says what the term means, not what the project
  decided about it.
- **Consistency.** Where two of the orrery's words already differ for
  one thing (piece C of C/2025 K1 read "Keplerian orbit" where its
  siblings read "near-parabolic", Tony's note of Oct 24: "same
  thing"), the JPL word is the one kept, and the other becomes its
  gloss or goes.
- **Scope.** Every served row, hover, text box and i panel, in the
  orrery and on the website; applied forward to every string a build
  touches, and recorded by class where an instance is found outside
  the slice (The Braid).

This sits beside, and does not weaken, the earlier ruling that
"hyperbolic" is explained as an open path (L-470, Oct 24): that ruling
was about adding the gloss; this one is about never dropping the word.
