2. Findings
 * The scene resolver currently forbids mixing bodies stored around different centres in one scene, which blocks Ruling 7 ("from the Sun, the root, everything mixes") whenever a visitor ticks an orbiting moon in the Sun-centred view.
   The resolver (the Python module that checks a scene request before anything is drawn) compares each ticked body's stored_center against the scene's center and aborts with a FrameRejectionError if they do not match. Because the Moon is stored around "earth", Io around "jupiter", and Titan around "saturn", the Sun-centred scene refuses to include them, and the assembler (the Python module that builds the 3D lines and markers) has no step that adds a parent planet's position to an orbiting child's ellipse and marker.
   * VERIFIED: gallery/assembler/resolver.py (resolve, if stored_center != center: raise FrameRejectionError) and gallery/assembler/assemble.py (assemble_scene).
 * Focusing on a body ("Go To, today") does not move the viewing box to that body's position; it always frames a cube around (0, 0, 0), which cannot zoom in on off-centre details like comet features, flybys, or moons in the Sun-centred view (Ruling 2).
   In Plotly (the 3D drawing library the page uses), the viewing box is the cube of space (xaxis.range, yaxis.range, zaxis.range) shown on screen, and the camera centre (scene.camera.center) is the pivot point the view turns around. Today, tapping "Go To" calls sunFocusOn and sunFrameOn, which leaves the box centred on (0, 0, 0) with edges at [-r, r], where r is the body's distance from the Sun or the width of its whole orbit around the Sun. If a visitor in the Sun-centred view focuses on Earth's shells, the Moon, or a comet feature at 1 AU from the Sun, a box spanning [-1.2 AU, +1.2 AU] around the Sun leaves a 10,000 km feature smaller than a thousandth of a pixel. Shifting the box to [x - h, x + h] around the focused body's coordinates [x, y, z] will also require updating the origin axis lines and tick marks (sunOriginAxisPoints, sunOriginTickPoints), which currently draw only along lines through (0, 0, 0).
   * VERIFIED: interactive.html (sunFocusOn, sunFrameOn, sunGroupRadius, sunPositionRadius, ssGo, sunOriginAxisPoints, sunOriginTickPoints).
 * Adding a parent's worked-out position to a child's shell or orbit (Rulings 5 and 7) is visually sound when viewing the parent and its own children together, but it will show visibly wrong if a visitor zooms in on a spacecraft flyby in the Sun-centred view.
   An osculating orbit (the simple two-body ellipse that matches a planet's position and velocity at one instant) slowly wanders away from JPL's full gravity calculation over time, an error the cache measures as drift. When the browser carries a planet's shells or moons out to the planet's place by adding the planet's worked-out position, the planet marker, its shells, and its moon orbits all share the exact same planet offset, so they never drift apart from one another. However, a spacecraft's path (positions/<slug>.json) is fetched directly from JPL around the Sun with no two-body drift. During a close flyby--such as the Psyche spacecraft passing 8,000.4 km from Mars on May 15, 2026, inside Phobos's 9,376 km orbit--Mars's worked-out position on today's ellipse differs from Mars's position on the flyby date (and even on the flyby date, two-body drift over weeks is thousands of kilometres), so in the Sun-centred view the spacecraft line will visibly miss Mars and Phobos unless the visitor opens the Mars-centred encounter view (Ruling 4).
   * VERIFIED (code and drift rates) / INFERRED (visual offset at flyby scale): gallery/assembler/render_orbits.py (propagate_marker, drawn_orbit_offset), gallery/assembler/render_spacecraft.py (build_spacecraft_traces), and data/solar-system/coverage_index.json (objects.mars.trust, objects.psyche_spacecraft.positions).
 * The global date-trust check (served_window) in the resolver will reject every historical encounter and any future encounter outside the current 88-day window (Ruling 4).
   The nightly builder computes one global served window (served_window, currently about 88 days on either side of today) from all sun-orbiting objects and writes it at the top of coverage_index.json. In resolver.py, resolve checks whether the scene's date falls inside that single window before checking which centre or objects were requested, and aborts with OutOfServedWindowError if the date lies outside it. Opening the Psyche Mars flyby on May 15, 2026, Artemis II in April 2026, or Apollo 11 in July 1969 will fail immediately in resolve unless encounter scenes validate against the encounter's own served window instead of the today-anchored global served_window.
   * VERIFIED: gallery/assembler/resolver.py (resolve, OutOfServedWindowError), tools/gallery_cache_builder.py (derive_served, TRUST_WINDOW_PARTICIPANT_FRAME), and data/solar-system/coverage_index.json (served_window).
 * The Solar System room currently drops all shell features and passes only the Sun at [0, 0, 0] to the feature renderer, while directional shells and Earth's derived guides assume a single body at the origin (Rulings 5 and 7).
   In interactive.html, SOLAR_SYSTEM_DRIVER does not return result.report["features"], and EXHIBITS["solar-system"].compose never calls GalleryFeatures.buildFeatureTraces. While buildFeatureTraces(featureRequests, bodies, opts) in gallery/feature_renderers.js already accepts a [x, y, z] position for each body in bodies to shift spherical and tilted shells (center = body.position), renderMagnetosphere takes only a single scene-wide opts.sunDir vector rather than computing the Sun direction [-x, -y, -z] from each planet's position, and gallery/earth_geometry.js (build, composeScene) hardcodes [0, 0, 0] for Earth's rotation axis, equator ring, Sun-direction line, and day-night terminator line.
   * VERIFIED: interactive.html (SOLAR_SYSTEM_DRIVER, EXHIBITS["solar-system"].compose), gallery/feature_renderers.js (buildFeatureTraces, renderMagnetosphere), and gallery/earth_geometry.js (build, composeScene).
 * The September 7 sizing assumption for Earth-Moon Lagrange points (Ruling 6, L-297) no longer holds because the Earth and Solar System rooms now draw the current minute (epochNow: true) instead of 00:00 UTC.
   Lagrange points are the five balance points where the gravity of two orbiting bodies lets a small craft keep pace with them. The sizing note (SIZING_earth_moon_lagrange_20260907.md, Option A) relied on the scene epoch and the nightly build's as_of_today vector both being 00:00 UTC on the same day. Since September 26-27, 2026, both the Solar System room and the Earth room set epochNow: true (interactive.html), propagating the Moon forward to the minute the visitor opens the page. Because the Moon moves about 13 degrees per day (~50,000 km), drawing the Earth-Moon Lagrange points from a frozen 00:00 UTC snapshot (as_of_today) while the Moon is drawn up to 24 hours later will leave L1 and L2 visibly off the Earth-Moon line by up to 13 degrees unless the builder serves a short multi-point vector span around today that the assembler can interpolate to resolved_epoch_jd.
   * VERIFIED: documentation/SIZING_earth_moon_lagrange_20260907.md ("One thing that helps", "Option A"), interactive.html (EXHIBITS.earth.epochNow, EXHIBITS["solar-system"].epochNow, EPOCH_ISO), and tools/gallery_cache_builder.py (as_of_today_km).
 * The nightly cache builder (tools/gallery_cache_builder.py) enforces three serving shapes and Earth-only pole queries today, so Mars, Pluto's binary system, Lagrange points, and encounters each need specific builder additions.
   In tools/gallery_cache_builder.py, assert_structural aborts the build if any entry does not match one of three shapes: osculating (elements around stored_center), positions (a path file for spacecraft or open-path comets), or features-only (the Sun). Supporting the full tree requires four concrete builder and config changes:
   * Mars centre (@499): phobos (401) and deimos (402) must be keyed in celestial_objects.py and added to data/objects_config.json with parent: "mars" and canonical_center: "@499" (neither is in objects_config.json or coverage_index.json today).
   * Pluto centre (@9): In data/objects_config.json today, pluto_barycenter has parent: "sun", pluto has parent: "sun" and canonical_center: "@9", and charon has parent: "pluto" and canonical_center: "@9"; for the Pluto node in the tree to centre on the barycentre (@9, per L-150), pluto and charon (and later Nix, Hydra, Kerberos, Styx) need to sit under pluto_barycenter rather than pluto under sun and charon under pluto.
   * Lagrange points (@399 and @sun): A fourth serving shape in process_object, serve_last_good, and assert_structural (which otherwise aborts on #3 non-spacecraft missing osculating) for targets 3011-3015 around @399 and Sun-Earth 31-35 around @sun.
   * Encounters and poles of date: Today a spacecraft has only one heliocentric positions file (positions/<slug>.json, around @sun), moons have only today's osculating elements, and fetch_pole_radec / fetch_orbit_plane hardcode Earth (POLE_QUERY['target'] = 399, ORBIT_QUERY['target'] = 3). An encounter (such as psyche_spacecraft at Mars on 2026-05-15) needs a planet-centred positions file (@499) for the spacecraft over the encounter window AND elements or positions for the planet's moons (phobos, deimos @499) anchored at the encounter date (since Phobos's 0.32-day orbit has a trust window of only ~1 hour around its epoch).
   * VERIFIED: tools/gallery_cache_builder.py (process_object, fetch_pole_radec, fetch_orbit_plane, assert_structural), data/objects_config.json (objects), and data/solar-system/coverage_index.json (objects).
 * When an orbiting moon is ticked in the Sun-centred view (Ruling 7), the distance and precision routine (SolarSystemFigures.distanceLine) will compute its distance from the Sun using its Earth-centred error unless told which centre to report against.
   In gallery/solar_system_figures.js, distanceLine measures the marker's [x, y, z] distance from (0, 0, 0) and rounds it using the body's single trust block and position_accuracy. If the Moon is placed at r_Earth + r_Moon in the Sun-centred view, [x, y, z] is ~1 AU from the Sun, but the Moon's served trust rate and position_accuracy are measured around Earth (@399).
   * VERIFIED (code behavior) / QUESTION (Tony's -- what the project should be): When a visitor ticks an orbiting moon (like the Moon or Phobos) while viewing from the Sun, should its text box still print its distance from its parent planet (as it does when centred on the parent), or its distance from the Sun (which would require combining the parent's heliocentric error and the moon's parent-relative error)? gallery/solar_system_figures.js (distanceLine) and gallery/earth_geometry.js (moonBox).
 * The desktop orrery's "Look Toward" camera (add_look_at_object_buttons, Ruling 8) sets the camera pivot (center) on the distant target body, which breaks turntable dragging and arrow-key stepping on the website unless adapted.
   In visualization_utils.py, add_look_at_object_buttons places scene.camera.eye near the origin (0.001 units along the line of sight) and sets scene.camera.center to the target body's [x, y, z] coordinates. On the website (interactive.html), touch dragging, navCameraStep (the four arrow buttons), and sunHudUpdate all rotate eye around camera.center assuming camera.center is {x: 0, y: 0, z: 0}; if camera.center is moved out to the target body while eye is at the origin, a finger drag or arrow tap swings the central body out of the view.
   * VERIFIED (code behavior) / QUESTION (method -- how the work is done): Should "Look Toward" on the website place camera.center at a small offset in front of eye near the origin (or lock turntable orbit around the origin) so finger rotation and arrow buttons turn the sightline around the central body? visualization_utils.py (add_look_at_object_buttons) and interactive.html (navCameraStep, NAV_SUN_CAMERA).

The Sun and Earth rooms leave ssDrawer null: they have no search box, no foldable groups, no fixed row for the central body (Earth has no fixed Earth row, as noted in L-514 Ruling 2), and tapping a shell's name moves the camera immediately. Replacing isolated rooms with one tree in Step 2 means SolarSystemDrawer must also handle shell sub-groups, derived guides (earth_geometry.js), and a fixed row for whichever body is currently the centre.
   * VERIFIED: interactive.html (buildSunDrawer, ssBuildRows, ssRender, ssSelect, ssReset) and gallery/solar_system_drawer.js (rowsFromServed, tickable, roomFor).
 * Ruling 1 and Ruling 3 conflict on what happens to a planet's "neighbourhood children" (which orbit the Sun) when the visitor centres on that planet.
   Ruling 1 says a neighbourhood child (such as the Aten asteroids under Venus, or missions and near-Earth asteroids under Earth) "still goes around the Sun and is only placed there for the visitor", while Ruling 2 states "Centre: a body at the middle, everything drawn fetched around it" and Ruling 3 states "Once a body is the centre, the drawer lists its branch only." When a visitor centres on Earth, Earth is at (0, 0, 0), and Sun-centred orbits (like Apophis, Bennu, or Voyager 1) cannot be drawn around Earth without subtracting JPL answers.
   * QUESTION (Tony's -- what the project should be): When a visitor centres on a planet (such as Earth), should its Sun-orbiting neighbourhood children be omitted from the planet-centred drawer, or listed with a note that they are drawn in the Solar System (Sun-centred) view? data/objects_config.json (rooms.solar-system.drawer.rows) and gallery/assembler/resolver.py (resolve).

3. View of the build order
The four-step order--(1) tree data, (2) unified drawer and centre switching for Sun and Earth, (3) Mars with Phobos and Deimos, (4) encounters--is the right sequence, provided Step 2 is split into two smaller steps (2a and 2b) and Step 1 keeps the existing rooms.solar-system.drawer.rows keys intact until Step 2 ships. In Step 2a, unify the drawer chrome (SolarSystemDrawer + buildSunDrawer), add the trail line, add the fixed centre row for Earth, and wire "Centre on <name>" for the Sun and Earth using their existing drivers and URLs (?exhibit=solar-system, ?exhibit=sun, ?exhibit=earth), which guarantees that every existing room keeps working for a visitor without touching the assembler's coordinate math. In Step 2b, add the cross-centre offset math in resolver.py, assemble.py, feature_renderers.js, and earth_geometry.js (carrying a child's shells and orbits to its parent's place by adding the parent's position, plus shifting sunFrameOn to focus on an off-origin body's [x, y, z] coordinates). Separating the drawer unification (2a) from the 3D coordinate translation and focus-box shift (2b) avoids debugging UI chrome and orbital geometry in the same Mode 5 pass, and gives Step 3 (Mars, Phobos, and Deimos) a proven template.
