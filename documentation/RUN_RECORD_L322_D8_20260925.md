## 1. Rulings this session

- **The tail is drawn round** (Tony, 2026-09-25, "Confirmed as
  recommended"). Slavin et al. (1985) measured the tail's width near the
  plane of Earth's orbit. The real distant tail is often flattened, in a
  direction set by the solar wind's magnetic field, which keeps changing
  (Sibeck and Lin 2014). Under average conditions GEOTAIL found it about
  as tall as it is wide (Maezawa et al. 1997). The rule is declared on
  `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_DRAWN_RADIUS_RADII` with that reason, and the hover
  says so.
- **The uncertainty reader goes into `constants_rows.py`, and the export
  serves it** (Tony, 2026-09-25, "okay. please continue", after asking
  what `constants_rows.py` is).

## 2. What the patch changes

Five files. The patch prints one `ok` line per file.

- `constants_new.py`: four new rows after `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_OBSERVED_RADII`.
  - `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_FLARE_END_RADII` = 120, measured, 2 figures,
    uncertainty 10, from Slavin et al. (1985).
  - `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_DIAMETER_RADII` = 60, measured, 2 figures,
    uncertainty 5, same source.
  - `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_DRAWN_RADIUS_RADII` = half the diameter, declared.
    It carries two rules: the tail is round, and its radius grows in a
    straight line from Shue's cut to the flare end, then stays constant.
  - `EARTH_MAGNETOTAIL_DRAWN_END_RADII` = the observed reach, 220,
    declared. The drawing stops where ISEE-3 stopped observing.
  - The observed row's stale note (the drawn tail was 100 Earth radii at
    line 765) is replaced, and it says its 100-120 is superseded by the
    1985 row. The 1983 misprint is quoted as printed on the flare-end row.
- `constants_rows.py`: reads the uncertainty field on a `# Figures:` line
  once, as `Row.uncertainty`, with one pattern, `UNCERTAINTY_FIELD_RE`;
  and `uncertainty_of(name)` for displays.
- `test_derived_figures.py`: uses that pattern instead of its own copy.
- `export_constants.py`: every exported row carries `"uncertainty"`, the
  text the row writes, or null. Schema 3 to 4. The gallery's
  `pull_constants_export.py` accepts "schema 3 or later", and
  `mirror_constants.py` copies only value, unit and figures, so nothing in
  the gallery breaks; gallery patch 3 is where the gallery starts using it.
- `earth_visualization_shells.py`:
  - The magnetopause is Shue's own surface, from the nose to
    `EARTH_MAGNETOPAUSE_CUT_ANGLE_DEG`, evaluated from the coefficient rows
    exactly as the gallery room evaluates it.
  - The tail starts where the surface stops. Its start is computed, not
    typed: 20.1 Earth radii in radius, 11.6 behind Earth, at the declared
    solar wind. It widens in a straight line to 30 at 120 behind Earth,
    stays at 30, and stops at 220.
  - The five numbers chosen by eye are gone: the 12 and 10 widths and the
    tail's 100, 15 and 25. Earth no longer calls the shared
    `create_magnetosphere_shape`; the seven other bodies that use it are
    untouched.
  - Each belt's rings run evenly from its inner edge row to its outer edge
    row, and one more ring at the peak row is drawn brighter.
    `belt_thickness` is gone.
  - The magnetosphere's info marker moves off the nose, which sits on the
    Sun direction arrow, to 60 degrees round the surface, as in the
    gallery room.
  - The hover and the checkbox tooltip are rewritten (section 4).
- A new file, this run record, in `documentation/`.

What moves on screen: the magnetosphere's shape and length, the belts'
width, and the magnetosphere's cross marker. The bow shock does not
move.

## 4. The visitor-facing text, old and new

### The magnetosphere hover

Old:

    The tail is DRAWN to 100 Earth radii. It has been OBSERVED to at
    least 220 Earth radii, which is how far the spacecraft went rather
    than where the tail ends.

    The surface drawn through that nose is an approximation of the shape,
    not the model's own. A second fit, Jelinek et al. (2012), puts the nose
    about an Earth radius farther out, which is inside the scatter above.

    Source (tail): Slavin et al. (1983), Geophys. Res. Lett. 10:973 -- ISEE-3.

New:

    The Sun-facing surface is the model's own shape, drawn only as far round
    as the paper plots it. A second fit, Jelinek et al. (2012), puts the nose
    about an Earth radius farther out, which is inside the scatter above.

    Spacecraft found that the tail stops widening about 120 Earth radii
    behind Earth, plus or minus 10, and that beyond there it is about 60
    Earth radii wide, plus or minus 5. The tail is drawn widening in a
    straight line from where the surface stops out to 120 Earth radii, then
    at a constant width. The straight line is our choice; the measurements
    give only its two ends.

    The drawing stops at 220 Earth radii, which is how far the spacecraft
    went, not where the tail ends.

    The tail is drawn round. The real tail is often flattened, in a direction
    set by the solar wind's own magnetic field, which keeps changing. Under
    average conditions, measurements far down the tail find it about as tall
    as it is wide.

    Source (tail width, end of widening): Slavin et al. (1985),
    J. Geophys. Res. 90:10875 -- ISEE-3.
    Source (how far observed): Slavin et al. (1983), Geophys. Res. Lett. 10:973.
    Source (tail shape): Sibeck and Lin (2014), doi:10.1002/2013JA019471;
    Maezawa et al. (1997), Adv. Space Res. 20:949 -- GEOTAIL.

## 5. For the ledger, one row per class

- **The magnetosphere hover gives Earth radii without kilometres or AU.**
  True of the old lines and the new. The AU convention asks for km and AU
  beside every distance; each would need its own row in
  `constants_new.py`, with a declared figure count, as the standoffs got
  at C2. Not done in this build.
- **The drawn tail's growth differs from the 1983 abstract's "about 30
  percent".** Between 20 and 120 Earth radii behind Earth the straight
  line grows about 44 percent, and about 32 percent at the low end of the
  1985 width's uncertainty. The 1983 figure lies inside the 1985
  envelope, so the construction stands; the note is on the flare-end row.
- **The uncertainty field's pattern reads a sentence's full stop as a
  decimal point.** Every row in the store today avoids it; nothing checks
  that the next one will.
- **The per-frame size of Earth's magnetosphere rose by about 42
  percent** (section 3). A rendering choice, for Tony's eye in animation.
- **The shell tooltip copy in `shell_configs.py`** (dead data, kept in
  agreement with its live twin) says nothing about the tail and still
  says the belts are "drawn at the flux peak". Not changed; its sentence
  is not wrong, only less complete.
