## What this is, in one paragraph

A friend of Tony's, on a phone, scrolled the front page to the bottom
not knowing what to do, was pointed to Explore, closed the room's
first-visit note without reading it, turned the picture with a thumb,
and then: "now what?!" Most visitors do not know what an orrery is.
This session builds two things. In the Solar System room, a guide of
four steps that each ask for one action and wait for it, on a small
story: home, the Moon, then Halley's comet. On the front page, two
lines saying what an orrery is, and a choice: "Show me around", which
opens the room with the guide, or "Explore on my own", which opens it
without. One setting in the visitor's browser drives both, and a
Tutorial switch at the bottom of the front page turns it back on.

## The design, settled (L-527 holds every ruling with its time)

The mockup, a sketch with no real positions, is the Design canvas
"Orrery first-visit guide: four steps",
https://claude.ai/artifact/QEXpNBnj8YxDHRfwF4CKLt (private to Tony;
the session asks him for screenshots if it needs a screen). Its
words are below, and they are the words to build.

**The cards.** The yellow of the room's i note (`--accent`, dark
text), one at a time, each with "Step n of 4" and four dots, and
"Skip the guide" on every card. A card asks for one action. The next
card appears only when that action is done; if the visitor does
something else, the card stays and does not repeat. The cards give
instructions only: every fact stays in the boxes, which carry their
sources, so the guide adds no claim to source.

1. The room's plain opening. "This is the solar system today, seen
   from above. Drag with one finger to turn it." Done: a turn.
2. A ring on Earth. "Find our home planet: tap Earth, the blue dot in the
   ring."
   Done: Earth's box opens.
3. Pointing at the menu button: "Open the menu, bottom left." Done:
   the drawer opens. Then: "This is the menu. On Earth's row, tap
   Center on Earth to fly in." Done: the view is centred on Earth.
   Then, at the Moon's row: "Now tick the Moon, to see it go
   around Earth." Done: the Moon ticked.
4. "There's the Moon, going around Earth. Next, a comet." with a large
   button, "Take me there". Its tap: back to the Sun's view keeping
   the ticks (the trail line's "Solar system", L-515 ruling 20), Halley
   ticked, and the drawer shown for a moment folded open at Comets,
   Halley-type comets, Halley (`comets_all`, `comets_htc` in
   `data/objects_config.json`), then closed. Then a ring on Halley:
   "That long orbit is Halley's comet. Tap Halley, in the ring, to see
   where it is today." Done: Halley's box opens.
5. The end card, labelled "The end", all four dots, its button
   "Explore": "That's the orrery: the solar system as it is today,
   drawn from JPL's (NASA) data, updated every night. Explore on your
   own, or turn on Tutorial on the front page to see this again."
