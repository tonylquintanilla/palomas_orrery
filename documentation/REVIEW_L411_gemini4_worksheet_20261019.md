## The discovery rows: the held sentences

Re-opened here before anything is built on them:

- **D3, the chromosphere: a real disagreement.** The website says the
  temperature rises "from about 6,000 kelvin at the bottom to about
  20,000 at the top". NASA/Marshall's page, re-opened, says "from
  6000 degrees C to about 20,000 degrees C"; 6,000 degrees Celsius is
  about 6,270 K. NASA's Sun Fact Sheet (D. R. Williams, last updated 9
  May 2024), re-opened, gives "Temperature at top of photosphere:
  4400 K" and "Temperature at top of chromosphere: ~30,000 K", with the
  chromosphere "~2500 km" thick. So in kelvin the base is near 4,400
  and the top tens of thousands. The sentence needs new words, which
  are Tony's: they go on the next words list.
- **Gemini misquoted the Fact Sheet.** It quoted "Temperature in
  chromosphere: ~4300 - 8000 K" and a top of "~20,000 K", and an
  effective-temperature line spelled "Surfaceura". The page, re-opened
  twice, carries none of these: it says ~30,000 K at the top and has
  no chromosphere range line. Its other quotes from that page match.
  Recorded as an attribution error the re-open caught.
- **D1, the core.** Bahcall, Pinsonneault and Basu (2001), arXiv
  astro-ph/0010346, Table 5, re-opened: the standard model's centre at
  15.696 (million K) and 152.7 g/cm3. The Fact Sheet, re-opened: 1.571
  x 10^7 K and 162.2 g/cm3. Both support "about 15 million kelvin" and
  "about 150 times as dense as water"; the Fact Sheet's density reads
  nearer 160. A row for each can now be written.
- **D2, the radiative zone.** Bahcall et al. (2001), same paper: the
  temperature at the base of the convection zone today is 2.18 x 10^6
  K, supporting "about 2 million at the top". The 7 million near the
  core rests on NASA/Marshall and on Bahcall et al. (1995)'s table
  (7.9 million at 0.25 solar radii), the latter not re-opened.
- **D4, Parker's crossing.** Kasper, Klein, Lichko and others,
  "Parker Solar Probe Enters the Magnetically Dominated Solar Corona",
  Phys. Rev. Lett. 127:255101 (2021), re-opened at the NSF's open copy
  (par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10319180): at 09:33 UT on 28 April 2021,
  13 million km above the photosphere, moving from 19.7 to 18.4 solar
  radii from the Sun's centre (Table I: 19.8 to 18.4). This agrees with
  ALFVEN_SURFACE_RADII, 19.7, and sources the sentence's "April 2021".
  NASA's announcement says "18.8 solar radii ... above the solar
  surface", which mixes centre and surface; the paper is the source.
- **D5, the Hills cloud model.** Nesvorny et al., posted 16 February
  2025, published ApJ 983:74; "inclined i ~ 30 degrees to the
  ecliptic", nearly polar in the Galactic frame. Already the project's
  cited source; the year can print from the citation.
